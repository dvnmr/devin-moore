Systems — ongoing

CORTEX — an operating system for thought

CORTEX started as a continuity system: I was tired of losing work — chats that forget, tools that can’t see each other, ideas stranded on a dead phone. It grew into a governed pipeline on my own laptop: request → retrieval → policy → action → verification → signed receipt → visual state.

  • Typed CLAIM records. Every load-bearing claim carries an ID, evidence list, confidence, status, and a calibration block separating what was verified by running from what is assumed or still open.
  • A deterministic Rust validator (rules R01–R10, no network, no AI in the loop) is the only component allowed to say “verified.” Models propose; the validator disposes.
  • Proof-ladder discipline. Idea, spec, code, test, merge, transfer, run-on-target-hardware, live, evaluated — no rung gets claimed without its own evidence.
  • Formal verification, reported honestly. Of 19 Kani proof harnesses on the OS kernel code, 13 proofs pass; 6 time out and are recorded as inconclusive — not success. An earlier “verified” label that outran the evidence was retracted in writing.
  • A bootable OS and an honest cockpit: where data can’t be reached, the interface shows UNKNOWN instead of inventing a number.

Method

How the crew works

Multiple AI models, each with a defined seat and a bounded job, coordinated through shared records: instant coordination in chat, a full backup of every seat’s work to shared storage on a twenty-minute cycle, and one rule above all — a claim moves forward only with its evidence attached. When a model’s own threshold math was tested and failed, the failure was published to the whole crew the same morning, formula and all. That is the culture the portfolio runs on.
